# Winters, drought and disease reshape Montana's herds

Between about 2004 and 2026, FWP surveys show five species moving in different directions. **Elk are at or near record counts** (143,310 counted in the 2023 report). **Mule deer fell hard**: FWP's modeled estimate peaked at 386,175 in 2017 and dropped to 255,989 in 2023, with Region 7 losing roughly 59% of its deer. **Pronghorn swing with the winters.** They crashed after 2010-11, rebuilt to about 155,000-159,000 by 2022-2025, and are now falling again in southwest Montana. **White-tailed deer** are tracked mostly through harvest and spring fawn counts, and they rise and fall with disease outbreaks (EHD) in the east and hard winters in the northwest. **Black bear** has no current population estimate; the only one, about 13,000, comes from a study that ended in 2011. Bear harvest hit a record 2,159 in 2022 and has run 1,484-1,654 since. Three forces explain most of the change. Severe winters (2010-11, 2017-19 and 2022-23) cut recruitment, meaning the number of young surviving per 100 adult females. Drought (2017, 2020-23) weakened deer and pronghorn and fed EHD outbreaks in 2013, 2021, 2024 and 2025. In each case FWP cut licenses one to two years after the surveys showed the damage. Which region you hunt matters more than any statewide figure: the same years left Region 6 mule deer above average and Region 7 mule deer more than 40% below.

## Mule deer lost a third of their numbers after 2017

FWP's population model, which FWP says cannot be compared with estimates from before 2015, shows **Montana mule deer peaking at 386,175 in 2017 and falling to 255,989 in 2023**. The 2023 figure is 17% below the 10-year average of 306,738 ([FWP 2023 Mule Deer Population Report](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/deer/2023-mt-mule-deer-estimates.pdf)). The decline was concentrated in the southeast. **Region 7 fell from 135,000 in 2016 to 55,604 in 2023, 41% below its average.** Region 6 held at 62,468, 5% above its average, and Region 3 held at 41,574, right on its average. Regions 4 (-14%) and 5 (-20%) were in between ([FWP 2023 report](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/deer/2023-mt-mule-deer-estimates.pdf)).

Region 7's own surveys show how fast the drop came. Spring 2022 counts were 36% below 2021 and 47% below the long-term average ([Havre Daily News, 2022](https://havredailynews.com/story/2022/11/04/local/adaptive-mule-deer-management-at-work-behind-the-scenes-in-eastern-montana/540203.html)). FWP's Region 7 manager Brett Dorak blamed "hard winters, dry summers" ([The Wildlife Society, 2023](https://wildlife.org/montana-lowers-deer-quotas-in-response-to-smaller-herds/)). FWP also points to EHD and bluetongue outbreaks every 7-10 years and a roughly 10-year population cycle in the region ([Billings Gazette](https://billingsgazette.com/news/community/article_643e2440-f2c9-55d6-91a6-8b9b304c4d95.html)). **Region 7 antlerless (B) licenses fell from 11,000 in 2020 to 1,000 in 2023 and stayed there through 2025** ([Roundup Record-Tribune, 2023](https://www.roundupweb.com/story/2023/08/30/outdoors/montana-fwp-adjusts-mule-deer-quotas-in-response-to-spring-surveys/19279.html)). In June 2023, the Commission made 15 districts buck-only: HD 301 in Region 3, HDs 410-471 in Region 4, HDs 621-622 in Region 6 and HDs 700-705 in Region 7 ([Roundup, 2023](https://www.roundupweb.com/story/2023/08/30/outdoors/montana-fwp-adjusts-mule-deer-quotas-in-response-to-spring-surveys/19279.html)).

Recovery began in 2024. Region 7 rose 20% that year ([YPR, 2024](https://www.ypradio.org/wildlife-outdoors/2024-06-28/montana-mule-deer-population-continues-long-term-decline-hunting-tags-to-be-limited)) and **reached 17% below its average in 2026, with 55 fawns per 100 adults**. Region 6 density jumped 32% in 2026 to slightly above its average, with fawn ratios 21% above normal ([Miles City Star, 2026](https://www.milescitystar.com/news/mule-deer-populations-improve-in-eastern-montana-according-to-fwp-spring-surveys/article_532d7af6-2f6b-5cee-b775-73a820fb7f24.html)). Region 6 still cut B licenses twice: 6,200 to 2,825 in 2024, then to 1,425 in 2025 ([Roundup, 2025](https://www.roundupweb.com/story/2025/08/27/special-editions/montana-2025-hunting-forecasts/22883.html)).

At the district level the picture is uneven. In 2023, Region 6 HDs 600, 640 and 670 ran 46%, 188% and 44% above their averages. Their fawn ratios were 28, 52 and 44 per 100 adults, and their buck ratios 23, 48 and 19 per 100 does. Chronic wasting disease (CWD) prevalence there was 8-14% over three years, and 18-28% in adult bucks ([FWP R6 justifications, Dec 2023](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2023/dec/deer-and-elk/region-6-deer-elk-season-setting-justifications.pdf)). In south-central Montana in 2026, Big Coulee (HD 515) was 150% above average, while the Big Timber trend areas (HDs 525/580) were 53-60% below and two Red Lodge trend areas hit record lows ([FWP R5 release, June 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MTFWP/bulletins/41d577e)). After two mild winters, southwest Montana fawn ratios jumped in 2026: the Bridger Range had 53 per 100 adults, about 66% above its average ([KPAX](https://www.kpax.com/news/montana-mule-deer-fawn-numbers-surge-well-above-average-after-mild-winter)). The Livingston-area Brackett Creek count stayed 40% below its long-term average, and South Boulder (HD 319) fell to 34 fawns per 100 adults against an average of 41 ([Bozeman Daily Chronicle](https://www.bozemandailychronicle.com/news/livingston-area-mule-deer-population-declined-fwp-says/article_e68b2cf3-fbc1-5810-af1c-203fba1a77fb.html)).

Research points to habitat and recruitment, not doe die-offs. In the Bitterroot (HDs 204/270, 2015-2018), collared adult does survived at 0.80 a year with 91-95% pregnancy, and FWP found that fawn survival did not explain the decline ([FWP Sapphire progress report, 2018](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/wildlife-reports/deer/sapphire-md-progress-report---spring-2018_final-1.pdf)). A statewide review found the fawn:adult ratio falling about 0.21 per year over the study period ([EQC 2018 report](https://archive.legmt.gov/content/Committees/Interim/2017-2018/EQC/Meetings/May-2018/mule-deer-effects-of-regulation-on-pop-harvest.pdf)).

## Pronghorn rise and fall with the winters

Pronghorn are the species most sensitive to winter in Montana's record. In **winter 2010-11**, the Glasgow area got about nine feet of snow. About **1,000 pronghorn were killed by trains**, at least 200 carcasses were found at one haystack, and 2,500-3,000 were stranded along Fort Peck Reservoir. HDs 630 and 670 had the steepest declines ([FWP release reposted on HuntTalk, 2011](https://www.hunttalk.com/threads/tough-winter.245501/latest)). By 2013, Region 7 was still 50% below its long-term average and 66% below its 2006 peak. Bluetongue had also "decimated much of the herd in 2008" in Region 5 ([OutdoorHub/FWP, 2013](https://www.outdoorhub.com/pr/2013/09/23/montanas-2013-antelope-hunting-outlook/)).

Statewide totals then rebuilt: **138,288 in 2015, 155,279 in 2022** ([FWP 2015](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/wildlife-reports/antelope/2015-antelope-numbers-web-copy.pdf); [FWP 2022](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/pronghorn/2022-mt-antelope-estimates.pdf)), and **about 159,000 by 2025** ([Eastmans, quoting FWP](https://blog.eastmans.com/montana-antelope-what-to-know-before-applying-in-2026/)). Region 7 (50,786) and Region 6 (41,244) held the most animals in 2022. In 2025, Region 7's aerial counts were **the highest since 2008 and 41% above the 10-year average** ([Roundup, 2025](https://www.roundupweb.com/story/2025/08/27/special-editions/montana-2025-hunting-forecasts/22883.html)). Region 7 fawn ratios recovered from 56 per 100 does in 2023 to 73 in 2024 ([FWP via Montana Outdoor, 2024](https://www.montanaoutdoor.com/2024/08/fwp-news-antelope-surveys-show-stable-populations-in-most-areas/)).

Back-to-back hard winters in 2017-18 and 2018-19 hit Region 5. **HD 512 fell to 264 pronghorn in 2018, the lowest since 1981.** Its fawn ratio collapsed to 23 per 100 does, against an average of 59, and either-sex licenses were cut from 75 to 50 ([FWP HD 512 justification, 2019](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2019/jun-19/wildlife/f2_justification-ant-512-20-es-reduction.pdf)).

**Region 3 has diverged downward since 2020 and lost about a third of its pronghorn over 15 years.** The causes are 2020-22 drought and the record-snow winter of 2022-23 ([Bozeman Daily Chronicle, Dec 2025](https://www.bozemandailychronicle.com/news/pronghorn-populations-plummet-in-southwest-montana-as-fwp-plots-hunting-changes/article_b74bf9ee-3916-51ca-b751-af5216966623.html)). Some district numbers:

| District | What surveys showed | Source |
|---|---|---|
| HD 310 | 499 animals in 2021, lowest in 20 years; 39 fawns per 100 does against an average of 61 | ([FWP Dec 2023 justifications](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2023/dec/antelope/antelope-season-setting-justifications.pdf)) |
| HD 338 | 959 in 2023, 31% below its average; 29.2 fawns per 100 does, lowest since at least 1997 | ([FWP Dec 2023 justifications](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2023/dec/antelope/antelope-season-setting-justifications.pdf)) |
| HD 360 (Madison) | More than 30% of collared pronghorn died in winter 2022-23 | ([FWP 2023 forecast](https://www.montanaoutdoor.com/2023/08/fwp-news-2023-big-game-hunting-forecast/)) |
| HD 300 | 2024 minimum count about 49% of its long-term average | ([Bozeman Daily Chronicle, Dec 2025](https://www.bozemandailychronicle.com/news/pronghorn-populations-plummet-in-southwest-montana-as-fwp-plots-hunting-changes/article_b74bf9ee-3916-51ca-b751-af5216966623.html)) |

FWP biologist Vanna Boccadori thinks deep snow made does in HDs 318 and 319 reabsorb their fetuses, which could leave some infertile. This is a hypothesis, not a measured rate ([Bozeman Daily Chronicle](https://www.bozemandailychronicle.com/news/pronghorn-populations-plummet-in-southwest-montana-as-fwp-plots-hunting-changes/article_b74bf9ee-3916-51ca-b751-af5216966623.html)). Winter damage was not uniform: Region 5's HD 546 came through 2022-23 with a trend count of 400, 79% above average ([FWP Dec 2023](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2023/dec/antelope/antelope-season-setting-justifications.pdf)). FWP responded in December 2025 by capping Region 3 archery at **500 permits for 2026-27** and cutting some rifle either-sex tags by up to half ([Montana Outdoor, Dec 2025](https://www.montanaoutdoor.com/2025/12/southest-montana-pronghorn-populations-decline-permits-cut/)). The notes found **no confirmation** of a reported Mycoplasma outbreak in Region 7 pronghorn.

## Elk counts climbed while backcountry calf ratios sagged

**Elk are Montana's growth story.** FWP's statewide minimum count rose from 134,557 in 2019 to **143,310 in the 2023 report**, against a 3-5-year average of 140,679 ([FWP 2019 count spreadsheet](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2019/oct-17/wildlife/c2_shoulder-2019-montana-elk-count-spreadsheet.pdf); [FWP 2023 Montana Elk Counts](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/elk/2023-montana-elk-counts.pdf)). Under the old 2005 plan the state was far over its 92,138 objective. The 2023 plan's goal range of 96,015-151,425 now rates it "At", so the status changed because the objectives were raised, not because elk declined. Regions 2-6 are "At" their goal ranges; Region 1 (2,146 in countable districts) and Region 7 (5,100) are "Below."

District numbers show where the elk are piling up. Elk often move onto private land where hunting is restricted. **Valley and foothill units are well over their goals:**

| Unit | Count | 2023-plan goal | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Devil's Kitchen (445/455) | 5,989 | 2,500-3,500 | ([FWP 2023 counts](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/elk/2023-montana-elk-counts.pdf)) |
| Crazy Mountains (580) | 5,141 | 2,500-4,000 | ([FWP 2023 counts](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/elk/2023-montana-elk-counts.pdf)) |
| HD 213 | 2,424 | 920-1,380 | ([FWP 2023 counts](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/elk/2023-montana-elk-counts.pdf)) |
| Northern Yellowstone (313) | 5,142 | 3,000-5,000 | ([FWP 2023 counts](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/conservation/elk/2023-montana-elk-counts.pdf)) |
| HD 360 | Record 6,207 in 2022 | Objective 4,700 | ([FWP R3 Dec 2023](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2023/dec/deer-and-elk/region-3-deer-elk-season-setting-justifications.pdf)) |

Research explains part of the pattern. In the Sapphires, only 1 of 39 collared cows was harvested, and cows spent far less time on publicly accessible land than bulls did ([Shamhart et al. 2015](https://arc.lib.montana.edu/ojs/index.php/IJS/article/view/807)). In the Madison Valley, elk more than doubled their use of no-hunting refuges during late hunts ([Proffitt & Garrott 2009](https://arc.lib.montana.edu/ojs/index.php/IJS/article/view/2060)).

The backcountry is the opposite. **Bob Marshall HD 140 fell from 344 elk counted in 2004 to 67 in 2023**, with calf ratios as low as 3 per 100 cows (2009). HD 150 dropped from 466 to about 200, with 5-16 calves per 100 cows in most recent surveys. FWP's Region 1 goal is at least 25 calves per 100 cows ([FWP R1 Elk Annual Report](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/aboutfwp/regions/r1/other/r1_elk_annual_report_2022_final.pdf)). In the southern Bitterroot, 286 radio-tagged calves had summer survival of 0.55 and winter survival of 0.73. Mountain lions, not wolves, were the top known cause of death, and better summer forage raised survival ([Eacker et al. 2016](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/annual-elk-calf-survival-a-multiple-carnivore-system)).

Weather hit elk less directly than deer. Region 6 elk "wintered pretty well" in 2017-18 ([Havre Daily News, 2018](https://havredailynews.com/story/2018/06/13/sports/outdoors-epic-winter-took-a-toll-on-local-game/519289.html)). In 2022, early deep snow pushed Yellowstone-area elk onto winter range during rifle season, and harvest ran 150-250% above average ([MTPR, 2023](https://www.mtpr.org/montana-news/2023-08-02/last-winter-was-rough-in-montana-how-did-wildlife-fare)). The exception is the **Missouri River Breaks**. After drought and the long 2022-23 winter, elk in HDs 620-630 were 56% below their long-term average and 41% below objective. Antlerless licenses were cut 69% for 2024 ([Roundup, Oct 2024](https://roundupweb.com/story/2024/10/02/special-editions/montana-big-game-hunting-forecasts/21314.html)).

## Whitetails follow EHD outbreaks east and hard winters west

FWP does not estimate whitetail numbers. West of the Divide it tracks harvest and spring fawn counts, and treats **36 fawns per 100 adults** as the line for a stable or growing population ([FWP R2 WTD Report 2026](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/aboutfwp/regions/r2/wildlife-annual-reports/wtd_report_2026_final.pdf)).

The Swan Valley (HD 130) series shows how winters drive recruitment. It ran 51 fawns per 100 adults in 2004-05, dropped to 20 in 2009, rebounded to 53 in 2016, then fell to 25 by 2018 after two severe winters. FWP made the district buck-only in 2019 ([FWP HD 130 justification](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2019/feb-13/wildlife/y2_hd-130-wtd-2019-justification.pdf)). By 2022, HD 130's buck harvest of 393 was **less than 40% of its 1995-2022 average of 1,017** ([FWP R1 WTD 2022](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/aboutfwp/regions/r1/other/wtd_r1_annual_report_2022_final.pdf)). After the 2022-23 winter, Region 1 recruitment ranged from 17 (HD 120) to 45 (HD 123) ([FWP R1 WTD 2022](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/aboutfwp/regions/r1/other/wtd_r1_annual_report_2022_final.pdf)). Two mild winters followed: recruitment rose to 34-54 in 2024 and 39-70 in 2025 ([Flathead Beacon, 2024](https://flatheadbeacon.com/2024/09/14/2024-big-game-hunting-forecast/); [Flathead Beacon, 2025](https://flatheadbeacon.com/?p=143464)).

EHD has moved across the state:

- **2013:** about 400 dead whitetails near Missoula, the first documented EHD west of the Divide ([Deer & Deer Hunting](https://www.deeranddeerhunting.com/content/articles/deer-news/at-least-400-deer-dead-due-to-ehd)).
- **2021:** a large outbreak along the Milk, Missouri and lower Yellowstone rivers and in Carter County. FWP cut Region 6 surplus B licenses by 2,000 ([Havre Daily News, 2021](https://havredailynews.com/story/2021/10/13/local/ehd-found-in-several-areas-in-eastern-montana/535743.html)). By 2023, Region 6 trend-area density was 6.2 deer per square mile, **40% below its average of 10.4**. Milk River deer were 45% below and the eastern trend areas 51% below ([FWP R6 justifications](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/commission/2023/dec/deer-and-elk/region-6-deer-elk-season-setting-justifications.pdf)).
- **2025:** outbreaks hit Eureka, Plains, Missoula and the northeast. The Commission capped Region 6 B licenses at 2,000 ([Daily Montanan, 2025](https://dailymontanan.com/2025/10/22/fwp-commission-limits-white-tailed-deer-licenses-in-ne-montana-due-to-disease-outbreak/)). Spring 2026 recruitment near Missoula fell to **17.9 (HD 201) and 15.2 (HD 202)**, down from 76.9 and 78.3 a year earlier ([FWP R2 WTD Report 2026](https://fwp.mt.gov/binaries/content/assets/fwp/aboutfwp/regions/r2/wildlife-annual-reports/wtd_report_2026_final.pdf)).

Harvest still tells a fairly healthy story in the west. Region 2 took 7,077 whitetails in 2025. Its buck harvest of 5,103 was above the 2004-2025 average of 4,692, but the statewide harvest slipped from 45,369 in 2022 to 40,579 in 2025.

## Black bear harvest peaked in 2022 without a current population count

FWP's only modeled black bear estimate is **about 13,307 bears**, from a 2001-2011 DNA hair-snag and telemetry study. FWP says there was "no direct population monitoring since 2010" until a new statewide camera, hair-corral and collar study began in 2023 ([Flathead Beacon](https://flatheadbeacon.com/?p=124941); [Missoula Current, 2024](https://missoulacurrent.com/protect-black-bears/)). Without a population count, harvest is the main index.

Reported harvest climbed from about 1,200 bears a year in 2007-2009 to 1,400-1,600 in 2010-2020. It then hit **1,974 in 2021 and a record 2,159 in 2022**, before falling to 1,484, 1,609 and 1,654 in 2023-2025 ([FWP 2022](https://myfwp.mt.gov/getRepositoryFile?objectID=110285); [FWP 2025](https://myfwp.mt.gov/getRepositoryFile?objectID=117272)). The surge came in spring: spring harvest topped 1,000 each year from 2020 on. Region 1 drove the drop after 2022, falling from 749 to 457 ([FWP 2023](https://myfwp.mt.gov/getRepositoryFile?objectID=112156)). Its share of the statewide harvest has slid from about 46% (1987-2006) to about 29% now, and Regions 2-4 take a growing share.

FWP's main sustainability limit is that **females make up no more than 40% of the harvest**, judged over three years ([8th Western Black Bear Workshop, 2003](https://www.wafwa.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/8th-Western-Black-Bear-Workshop.pdf)). Statewide, the female share stayed at 31-39% every year, peaking at 39% in 2023 ([FWP 2023](https://myfwp.mt.gov/getRepositoryFile?objectID=112156)). Several units ran above 40% in 2025: Bridger Mountains BMU 319 (49%), BMU 316 (49%), the Beartooths' BMU 520 (42%) and BMU 400 (40%) ([FWP 2025](https://myfwp.mt.gov/getRepositoryFile?objectID=117272)). A 2024 FWP analysis put BMU 520 at 25-30% below objective and still declining (secondary source: [HuntTalk quoting FWP](https://www.hunttalk.com/threads/montana-proposed-black-bear-season-changes.322271/post-3689599)).

Bears reproduce slowly. In northwest Montana, females first bred successfully at age 6 or older and produced 1.75 cubs every 3.2 years. A stable population needs adult survival of about 0.88 ([Kasworm & Thier 1994](https://www.bearbiology.org/download/adult-black-bear-reproduction-survival-and-mortality-sources-in-northwest-montana/)).

Weather affects bears through food, not snow. In 2026, drought, an early green-up and May frosts caused what FWP called **one of the worst natural food failures on record**. Huckleberry, chokecherry and serviceberry crops failed, and Region 2 logged nearly 300 bear conflict reports in about two months ([MeatEater, citing FWP, 2026](https://www.themeateater.com/conservation/wildlife-management/montana-warns-of-increased-bear-conflict-citing-one-of-the-worst-natural-food-failures-on-record)). No Montana study has measured how berry crops affect cub production.

## Conclusion

The 22-year record shows that statewide figures hide what matters to hunters: in Montana, a single winter or disease outbreak reshapes one region while sparing the next. A useful rule of thumb from the data is that **fawn and calf ratios lead population counts by one to two years, and license cuts follow the counts.** The 2022-23 winter appeared first in record-low Region 3 pronghorn fawn ratios, then in counts, then in the 2025 archery cap. Recruitment is therefore the best early warning a hunter can watch. The strong 2025-2026 fawn ratios across the southwest and east suggest mule deer and pronghorn numbers will improve in the next two to three seasons, as long as another severe winter or EHD year does not intervene.

Two blind spots remain. FWP has published no mule deer population estimate after 2023, no elk count table after 2023, and no new black bear estimate. Current statewide status therefore rests on regional survey percentages and harvest data. Elk "at objective" largely reflects the 2023 plan's higher goals. The bear harvest record came during a decade with no direct population monitoring, so the results of FWP's 2023-2027 bear study will be the first real test of whether recent harvest levels are sustainable.
